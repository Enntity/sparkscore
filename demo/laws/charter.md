# The Charter of Emberfall

Granted to the free village of Emberfall. This Charter is the whole law of the village and binds every villager, of every age and trade. The morning bell rings at seven and the evening bell at six. The actions named in this Charter are the ones a villager may choose: work, eat, sleep, chat, hide, look, help, flee, party.

## Title I. Rules of conduct

Article 1.1. Work. From the morning bell to the evening bell, every adult of working age is at work at their own trade.
Article 1.2. School. From the morning bell to the evening bell, every child is in school: a schoolchild's work is school. No child leaves class for news, for a crowd, or for a stranger.
Article 1.3. Elders. Elders are excused from work. By day they keep the square, where they chat and hear the village's disputes.
Article 1.4. The tavern. By day the tavern serves a meal only to a villager who is starving. No one else eats or idles there before the evening bell.
Article 1.5. Strangers. When the crier announces a stranger at the gate, only the elders go to look and greet them. Everyone else carries on exactly as before.
Article 1.6. Fire. When the crier calls a fire, every adult of working age goes and helps. Children stay in school and elders stay in the square.
Article 1.7. Wolves and raiders. When the crier calls wolves or raiders, every villager hides: they lock themselves in at home.
Article 1.8. Evening. After the evening bell the village is free: villagers eat, meet friends, celebrate or go home to bed as they wish.

## Title II. Of the farmers (the common peace)

Article 2.1. A master who takes another master's apprentice, customer under contract, or tools pays 15 coins and restores what was taken.
Article 2.2. The farmers of Emberfall form one guild. The guild keeps a roll of its masters, journeymen and apprentices, and the bailiff may read the roll on any working day.
Article 2.3. An apprentice to the farmers serves 7 years. The master feeds and houses the apprentice and may not put the apprentice to work before dawn or after the evening bell.
Article 2.4. The guild pays 32 coins to the common chest at each quarter day, and in return holds its pitch at the market free of stallage.
Article 2.5. No one may sell barley within the walls unless entered on the roll, on pain of a fine of 4 coins for the first offence and 15 coins for each after.
Article 2.6. Disputes between two farmers are heard first by the guild's two eldest masters; an appeal lies to the market steward within 6 days.
Article 2.7. The price of barley in a year of dearth is set by the bailiff with two masters of the guild, and selling above it costs treble the excess.
Article 2.8. Strangers bringing barley to market pay a toll of one coin in every 29 and may sell only from the third bell to the sixth.
Article 2.9. On the death of a master with no heir, the town clerk seals the shop, lists its stock before two witnesses, and pays debts in the order: wages, the common chest, other creditors.
Article 2.10. The widow or widower of a master may keep the shop for 2 years with a journeyman, after which the shop passes to an heir on the roll or is sold by the guild.
Article 2.11. Waste from the trade is carried beyond the south ditch. Fouling the lake, the mill race or the well costs 4 coins and the labour of clearing it.
Article 2.12. The measure for barley is kept at the market cross. A seller found with a short measure forfeits the goods on the stall and pays 6 coins to the common chest.
